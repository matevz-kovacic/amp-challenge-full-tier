# Consensus AMP library: reproducible generation and diversity-aware selection

Participation requested: full requirements / co-authorship eligibility. This states the requested tier, not a claim of guaranteed qualification or authorship.

We prepared a reproducible generative submission by consolidating ten completed peptide-design runs. The runs comprise empirical sequence models, a hidden Markov model, a recurrent generator, synthetic-teacher-derived counts, and procedural sequence grammars. Their 500,000 records yield 499,998 distinct N-to-C peptide identities. All candidates received a common panel consisting of two related hemolysis classifiers and a physicochemical desirability heuristic. Average-tie Borda scores were averaged within evaluator families and then equally across families, producing a fixed ordinal quality ranking. Greedy Maximal Marginal Relevance selected 100 candidates from 10,000 reference-eligible consensus leaders; the selected candidates retain their original quality order. The 50,000-member library reserves these candidates and selects the remaining members proportionally across 256 length/composition clusters, with quality preference within each cluster. Pinned reference checks enforce sequence validity and shortlist novelty. A default inference entry point regenerates all ten source pools from frozen code, weights and seeds, then verifies their union and recomputes ranking and assembly from the frozen common scores and cluster assignments. The method offers reproducible computational candidates; its consensus scores do not establish antimicrobial efficacy, clinical safety, or competitive performance.

Public repository: https://github.com/matevz-kovacic/amp-challenge-full-tier. The repository provides the final 50,000-member library, ranked top100, all ten generative source implementations and weights, frozen common scores/cluster assignments, inference entry point, environment lockfile and reviewer transcripts.
